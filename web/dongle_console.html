Button mapping needs firmware v4

This dongle's firmware does not offer button mapping (it advertises no espp.gamepad_map module): it is older than v4, and applies the fixed stick / button options on the Settings tab instead. v4 decodes any BLE gamepad and remaps its buttons and axes per controller type. It also changes the dongle's flash layout, so moving to it takes one Full update over Web Serial (an OTA update cannot change the partition table); paired controllers and settings are kept.

Maps are not saved yet

This dongle runs v4+ firmware on the old flash layout (it was updated over USB, which cannot change the partition table), so it has nowhere to store button maps: edits apply until the next reboot at most. One Full update adds the storage; paired controllers and settings are kept.

Full update (Web Serial)

Writes the latest release's bootloader, partition table, OTA data and app at their flash offsets through the ESP32-S3's ROM download mode, with esptool-js over Web Serial. A dongle needs it once to move to firmware with a new flash layout (v3 → v4 adds the button-map storage): an OTA update can only replace the app. The chip is not erased: NVS keeps the paired controllers and the settings. Chrome / Edge only (Web Serial).

No published firmware here (the page was opened from a file, or nothing is published next to it). Pick the images of a release or a local build instead: build/bootloader/bootloader.bin, build/partition_table/partition-table.bin, build/ota_data_initial.bin, build/esp-usb-ble-hid.bin.

  1. Reboot the dongle into its ROM download mode: the console asks it to (the USB link drops). A dongle that does not answer: hold its BOOT button while plugging it in.
  2. Pick the ROM's serial port (Espressif, 303a:1001 “USB JTAG/serial debug unit” or 303a:0009) and flash; the dongle restarts into the new firmware and the console reconnects to it.
